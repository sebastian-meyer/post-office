The features described on this page are only available to administrators.

Domains

All domains are listed under "Manage". Click "New domain" to add a domain. Before the mail server can receive emails for a new domain, its MX record has to point to your mail server.

A domain can only be deleted when it has no accounts left. This makes sure that no accounts get lost by accident.

Under "Domain managers" on the page of a domain you can appoint users who are allowed to manage all accounts and mailing lists of that domain (see first steps).

DNS Check

"DNS check" on the page of a domain shows whether the DNS records of the domain are set up for sending and receiving emails with your mail server. Each check is marked as "OK", "Note" (optional or not checked), "Warning" (works, but could be improved) or "Error" (affects the delivery of emails), and shows the records found. For missing records, it suggests a record to add.

MX
The MX records must point to your mail server, and their hosts must have IP addresses.
Reverse DNS
The IP addresses of your mail server must point back to its host name. This is the same for all domains, and is set by the provider of the IP addresses, not in the domain's DNS zone.
DNSSEC and DANE
DANE lets other mail servers verify the certificate of your mail server with TLSA records. It requires the zone to be signed with DNSSEC.
SPF, DKIM and DMARC
These records protect your domain from forged senders. Without them, many mail servers reject your emails or mark them as spam. The SPF record must allow your mail server to send emails, a DKIM key must be published for the selector your mail server signs with, and the DMARC policy should be "quarantine" or "reject".
MTA-STS and TLS-RPT
Both are optional. MTA-STS tells other mail servers to deliver emails to your domain only encrypted, TLS-RPT asks them to report problems with that.

Some checks depend on settings of the Post Office (MAIL_HOSTNAME , DKIM_SELECTORS and DNS_RESOLVERS , see configuration) and are skipped if they are missing. Changes to DNS records can take a while to show up, depending on their TTL.

TLS Policies

By default, Postfix encrypts emails sent to other mail servers whenever possible (DANE or opportunistic TLS). TLS policies override this for specific recipient domains, e.g. to enforce encryption with a verified certificate for a business partner, or to disable encryption for a mail server with a broken TLS setup.

"Recipient domain"
The domain of the recipients, e.g. example.com . A leading dot (e.g. .example.com ) includes all subdomains.
"Policy"

One of the TLS security levels of Postfix:

none
Never encrypt.
may
Encrypt if possible, but don't verify the certificate.
encrypt
Always encrypt, but don't verify the certificate.
dane and dane-only
Verify the certificate with DANE, if possible (dane ) or always (dane-only ).
fingerprint
Always encrypt and verify the certificate's fingerprint, which has to be given in the parameters.
verify and secure
Always encrypt and verify the certificate against trusted certificate authorities.
"Parameters"
Optional parameters for the policy, e.g. match=hostname or match=AA:BB:CC:... for fingerprint . See the Postfix documentation for details.

Disabled TLS policies are ignored.

Blacklist

The mail server doesn't accept emails from senders on the blacklist.

"Sender"
An email address, e.g. spammer@example.com , or a domain, e.g. example.com . A domain includes all its subdomains.
"Action"
"Reject" refuses the email, so the sender is notified that it wasn't delivered. "Discard" accepts the email, but silently drops it, so the sender doesn't notice.

Disabled blacklist entries are ignored.

The blacklist checks the envelope sender of an email, which isn't necessarily the sender shown by email clients. It is meant to block persistent unwanted senders, not as a replacement for a spam filter.
