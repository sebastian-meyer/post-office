This page is meant for domain managers, list managers and regular users. Administrators can do everything described here for all domains.

Your Own Account

Every user can change their name under "Account settings" and their password under "Change password". The account settings also show the storage quota and all mailing lists you are a member of.

To leave a mailing list, click "Leave" next to it. Only a list manager can add you to the list again.

Accounts of a Domain

Domain managers find their domains under "Manage". The domain page lists all accounts of the domain with their type and status. From there you can

  • create new accounts (see first steps for the account types),
  • edit an account, e.g. to change the name, password or quota of a mailbox, or the destination of an alias,
  • disable and enable an account by clicking its status,
  • delete an account,
  • write an email to accounts of the domain (see Writing Emails).

To disable, enable or delete several accounts at once, mark them with the checkboxes in the first column and click "Enable", "Disable" or "Delete" above the table. The checkbox in the table header marks all accounts matching the current search. Accounts you aren't allowed to change (e.g. your own account or an administrator account) are skipped.

Disabling an Account

A disabled account can't log in to the mail server or the Post Office, and emails to it are rejected (or delivered to the catch-all address, if there is one). All its settings, emails and manager roles are kept, so it can be enabled again at any time.

Deleting an Account

Deleting an account removes it from all mailing lists and revokes all its manager roles. Aliases which only forwarded to the deleted account are deleted as well, because they would be useless otherwise.

Deleting a mailbox doesn't delete the emails stored on the server. Ask your server administrator to remove them (see maintenance), especially before a new mailbox with the same address is created.

You can't delete your own account or an administrator account.

Mailing Lists

List managers find their mailing lists under "Manage" in the section "Mailing lists I manage". Domain managers can open all mailing lists of their domains from the domain page.

Members

Under "Members" you can add and remove members by their email address. Members don't need an account in the Post Office and may belong to any other mail server. Users can't join mailing lists by themselves, but they can leave them.

To remove several members at once, mark them with the checkboxes in the first column and click "Remove" above the table. You can also write an email to the marked members, or to the whole list with "Write to the list" (see Writing Emails).

Settings

"Replies go to the list"
Adds a Reply-To header with the list address to every email, so replies are sent to all members instead of only the original sender.
"Members only"
Only members of the list are allowed to send emails to it. Emails from other senders are rejected.

List Managers

Domain managers can appoint list managers under "List managers". List managers can change the settings and members of their lists and delete them, but they can't appoint other list managers.

Writing Emails

Administrators and domain managers can write emails to the mailboxes, aliases and mailing lists of their domains, list managers to their mailing lists and the lists' members:

  1. Mark the recipients with the checkboxes in the table of the domain page or in the members table of the list page. The checkbox in the table header marks all entries matching the current search.
  2. Click "Write email" above the table (or "Write to the list" on the list page).
  3. Check the recipients, enter subject and message, and click "Send".

The email is sent from your own email address with your own account, just like an email from your mail program. The recipients don't see each other's addresses, and you get a copy of the email, because it isn't saved in your "Sent" folder.

Addresses which can't receive emails are left out, and the email form lists them: disabled accounts, send-only addresses, catch-all addresses, mailing lists without members, and "members only" lists you aren't a member of.

The Post Office keeps your password from the login (encrypted) until you log out, so it can send the email with your account. If you logged in before this feature was installed, or the mail server rejects the password, the email form asks for your password.

Catch-all Addresses

An alias or mailing list without a username is a catch-all address. It receives all emails to addresses of its domain which don't belong to any other enabled account. Each domain can have one catch-all address.

Catch-all addresses tend to receive a lot of spam, because spammers often send emails to random addresses.
